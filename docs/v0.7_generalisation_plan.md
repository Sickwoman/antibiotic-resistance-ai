# Version 0.7 plan: does the model survive a change of hospital or year? (fixed before any locked test part was scored)

Written on 2026-09-23. It applies the approved [evaluation protocol](evaluation_protocol.md). It spends the
two test parts that protocol decision 7 locked until this version, so everything that could be chosen after
seeing them is fixed here instead: which models are fitted, on which rows, with which settings, which test
parts they are scored on, and which comparisons are reported. The two things this adds to the protocol are
recorded there as [amendment 3](evaluation_protocol.md#amendments). Settings are in `config.yaml` →
`generalisation`.

## Goal

The specification calls this the project's central experiment, and states the research question it exists
to answer:

> "Can we create an antibiotic-resistance prediction model that remains reliable across different
> hospitals, time periods, instruments, and bacterial populations?"

It asks for a table comparing **random split** against **cross-hospital** against **temporal**
performance, and for the honest caveat that we may not claim the problem is solved:

> "Do not claim we solved this problem until experimental results prove it."

So Version 0.7 measures how much performance is lost when the test data comes from a different site or a
later year, and reports that loss with intervals. It does not attempt to *fix* the loss — adaptation is
Version 0.8, and nothing here may be changed because of what the numbers turn out to be.

## What this version may and may not change

- **It changes no model, no setting and no cut-off.** The hyperparameters are the ones Version 0.4 selected
  on the `random` split, reused verbatim (see below). The threshold rule is the approved one (highest
  threshold with validation sensitivity ≥ 0.90), applied to each experiment's *own* validation part.
- **It scores each locked test part once per model**, and appends every scoring to
  `results/experiments/test_evaluations.csv`, as protocol section 4 requires.
- **`random` and `within_year` are not scored again.** Their rows come from the existing log, written in
  Versions 0.3 and 0.4.
- No result here may motivate a change to this plan. If something looks wrong, it is reported as it is.

## Why the hyperparameters are not re-searched

Each experiment refits the **Version 0.4 winning LightGBM setting unchanged**:

```
num_leaves 31, n_estimators 300, learning_rate 0.1, colsample_bytree 0.3,
reg_lambda 10.0, min_child_samples 10, subsample 1.0, class_weight null
```

with the same sigmoid calibration on out-of-fold predictions of 5 patient-grouped folds of that
experiment's training part.

The reason is that the question is about the *data regime*, not about how well a search can do in it. If
each experiment got its own search, a gap between two experiments would mix two causes — different data and
different settings — and could not be read as generalisation loss. Refitting one fixed recipe leaves the
training and test data as the only thing that changed.

The cost of this choice is stated with the results: a setting chosen on `random` may suit the other regimes
less well, so the gaps reported here are **upper bounds on the loss attributable to domain shift under a
fixed recipe**, not the best achievable performance in each regime.

## The experiments

Every training part below is disjoint from its own test part; the row overlaps were checked on the real
dataset before this plan was written, and the one that matters is recorded in the table.

| # | Experiment | Train | Validation | Test | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | `random` | A, 2,977 | A, 426 | A, 856 | reference; **already scored in V0.4, reused** |
| E2 | `within_year` | A 2017, 1,284 | A 2017, 183 | A 2017, 366 | patient-overlap check; **already scored, reused** |
| E3 | `temporal` | A before 2017-10, 2,505 | A 2017-10 to 2017-12, 465 | A 2018, 1,233 | does a model trained on older data work later? |
| E4 | `external` | A, 3,831 | A, 428 | B 213 and D 1,938, **reported separately** | does a model trained at one hospital work elsewhere? |
| E5 | `external_ab` (new) | A + B, minus validation | 10 % of A + B, patient-grouped | D 1,938 | the specification's "train two sites, test a third" shape |
| E6 | saved project model | *none — already fitted* | *none — threshold 0.1426 kept* | B 213 and D 1,938 | does the model this project actually saved work elsewhere? |

**E6 needs no refit and is leakage-free.** The saved `v0.4.0-tuned_lightgbm-random-seed42` was trained on
the `random` training part, which is DRIAMS-A only. Its intersection with the `external` test part is **0
rows and 0 patient groups**, checked on the real dataset. It is *not* scored on the `temporal` test part,
because there the intersection is **848 of 1,233 rows** — that model has seen most of those spectra, and
scoring it there would be leakage, not generalisation.

**Seeds.** E3, E4 and E5 are refits with random training, so protocol decision 6 applies: seeds 42–46, mean
and range reported, bootstrap intervals for seed 42. E6 is the one saved model, so it has one row per test
site.

**Reference rows.** Each test part also gets the prevalence reference of protocol section 5 (a model always
predicting the training resistance rate). It needs no fit.

## D is deliberately scored under two training regimes

The D rows are the test part of both E4 (trained on A) and E5 (trained on A + B). That is the comparison
E5 exists for: does adding a second, small site to the training data help at a third site? Because both
use the **same 1,938 rows**, the difference is compared with a **paired** bootstrap (the same resamples for
both), which is the more sensitive test and is pre-specified here rather than chosen later.

This is a pre-specified comparison, not a repeated look for the better number. Both results are reported
whichever way the difference falls, and neither is allowed to become "the" external result afterwards.

## Pre-specified comparisons

1. **Generalisation gap**, as protocol section 3 already defines it: the `random` test AUROC minus the
   `temporal` test AUROC, and minus the `external` test AUROC **for each site separately**. These test sets
   share no rows, so the interval comes from the second-level unpaired bootstrap
   (`unpaired_difference`), which is wider than a paired one and is meant to be.
2. **Two sites versus one** (E5 − E4 on the same D rows): paired bootstrap of the difference, as above.
3. **Patient overlap** (E1 versus E2): already reported in Version 0.4; repeated in the Version 0.7 table
   so the three regimes can be read next to each other.
4. A gap is called real only when the 95 % interval of the difference excludes 0. A gap whose interval
   includes 0 is reported as "not demonstrated", never as "no difference".

## Do the Version 0.6 confidence zones survive a change of site?

Version 0.6 fitted a high-confidence susceptible zone on the `random` validation part: probability below
**0.1026**, which covered 25.8 % of those rows and was correct for 95.5 % of them, against a
pre-registered target of 95 %. There was no high-confidence resistant zone.

Version 0.7 applies **that same edge, unchanged**, to each new test part, and reports coverage and NPV with
patient-group bootstrap intervals. The edge is **not refitted** on the new sites: refitting would answer a
different question (can a zone be found there?) and would need that site's labels, which is exactly the
thing an external site does not give you in practice.

Pre-registered reading, fixed now:

- The zone **transfers** if its NPV at the new test part is ≥ 0.95, or its interval covers 0.95.
- It **does not transfer** if the interval lies wholly below 0.95. In that case the reported conclusion is
  that a confidence zone fitted at one site may not be assumed at another, and the honest consequence —
  that the zone would have to be refitted per site on local labelled data — is stated as future work, not
  done here.

## Why performance changes: a feature-only diagnostic

To describe the shift without using any AST label, and after every model result above has been logged:

- the per-bin mean intensity difference between the training site and each test site, summarised over the
  m/z range, and the **lowest bins reported separately** because DRIAMS-D spectra start near 2,000 Da while
  A and B start near 1,960 Da — a known site signature that has nothing to do with resistance;
- the share of the Version 0.6 top-20 regions whose intensity distribution differs most between sites, so a
  drop can be related to the regions the model actually relies on.

This uses test-set **inputs** but no test-set labels, and it is computed after the scorings are logged, so
it cannot influence any model or threshold. It is a description of the shift, not a correction for it.

## DRIAMS-C

The specification asks for "train A + B, test C". DRIAMS-C was not available in Versions 0.1–0.6, so the
third site has been DRIAMS-D. C is being downloaded for this version. Fixed here, before its contents are
seen:

1. **The pre-registered pair-selection rule is checked on C first**, as protocol section 3 requires. C is
   used only if *E. coli* × ciprofloxacin in C has enough resistant and susceptible samples under that same
   rule. If it does not, C is reported as unusable for this pair, with its counts, and nothing else changes.
2. **The primary dataset is not rebuilt.** C is built as a separate cohort with the *same* preprocessing
   settings, so it carries the same `feature_fingerprint` and a different `row_fingerprint`. The primary
   dataset's rows, its saved splits, the saved model and every logged test result therefore stay exactly as
   they are. Adding a site must not silently re-date five versions of results.
3. C is then a **further external test site**, treated exactly like B and D: scored once per model, reported
   separately, never pooled into a single "external" number.
4. If C has no patient identifiers, its intervals are sample-level, with the caveat amendment 1 point 2
   already fixed for B and D.

## What may not be claimed

- Not that the problem of cross-site generalisation is solved. Version 0.7 measures it.
- The `temporal` result is a **date-separated evaluation with incomplete patient linkage**, never a
  patient-level generalisation result (amendment 1, point 1). DRIAMS-A re-hashes patient IDs every year, so
  a returning patient cannot be detected across the boundary.
- External-site intervals are **sample-level with unknown within-patient dependence** (amendment 1, point
  2), and B, with 59 resistant isolates, is indicative only.
- A per-site difference is not an instrument effect, a population effect or a protocol effect unless
  something in the data shows which; the diagnostic above describes the shift, it does not attribute it.
- Nothing here is clinical evidence, and no output recommends an antibiotic.

## Reporting

- Every number comes from the saved run logs through a generator script; no metric is typed by hand.
- Every table shows n, the number of resistant isolates, and the interval.
- Every experiment records dataset name, both fingerprints, split, git commit, settings and seed.
- The test log is compared byte for byte before and after each run, and each run records its hash. Rows are
  only ever appended.
- Failed and worse-than-baseline results are reported, including the case where the model does no better
  than prevalence at a site.
