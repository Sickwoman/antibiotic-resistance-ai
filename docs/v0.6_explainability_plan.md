# Version 0.6 plan: explanations and confidence zones (fixed before any explanation was computed)

Written on 2026-09-20. It applies the approved [evaluation protocol](evaluation_protocol.md) and does not
change any decision in it; the one thing it adds to the protocol is recorded there as
[amendment 2](evaluation_protocol.md#amendments). Settings are in `config.yaml` → `explain`.

## Goal

Two things the specification asks for:

1. **Explain a prediction.** Show which parts of a spectrum pushed the model towards *resistant* or
   *susceptible* — globally, over the whole validation part, and for one individual spectrum.
2. **Stop forcing a call.** The model must be able to answer *uncertain* instead of R or S, with the
   boundaries derived from validation and calibration rather than chosen by hand.

## What is explained

The **project's model**: the Version 0.4 tuned LightGBM, saved as
`models/v0.4/ecoli_ciprofloxacin/best_random.joblib` (`v0.4.0-tuned_lightgbm-random-seed42`, cut-off
0.1426, test AUROC 0.751 [0.696, 0.807]). The Version 0.5 networks lost, so explaining them as the main
model would describe a model nobody uses; the MLP appears only as an independent cross-check (below).

Its feature space is the full 6,000 bins of 3.0 Da: **bin *j* is m/z [2000 + 3*j*, 2000 + 3(*j*+1))**. There
is no PCA and no feature selection in the winning pipeline, so every contribution maps to one real m/z
interval. The fitted model splits on 3,908 of the 6,000 bins.

## Methods (and why these)

| Method | What it gives | Why this one |
|---|---|---|
| **Exact TreeSHAP** | signed contribution of every bin to every individual prediction, additive: the contributions of one spectrum sum to its margin | LightGBM computes exact TreeSHAP itself (`pred_contrib=True`), verified here to reproduce the raw margin to 8.9e-16. No approximation and no extra dependency; the `shap` package would add a library across four CI jobs for a number we can already get exactly |
| **Block permutation importance** | AUROC drop when a contiguous block of 6 bins (18 Da) is permuted jointly | model-agnostic, and it answers a different question than SHAP (how much the model's *performance* relies on a region, not how much it *moves one prediction*). Neighbouring bins of one peak are strongly correlated, so permuting single bins understates a peak: the model reads the same peak from the neighbour |
| **Single-bin permutation** | the same for the top SHAP bins only | the cross-check that says whether a bin matters on its own or only as part of its neighbourhood |
| **m/z regions** | the reported unit: neighbouring important bins merged into one m/z interval | a 3 Da bin is below the resolution at which anything can be said; a region is the honest unit |

**Deliberately not used:** kernel SHAP and other model-agnostic approximations (exact TreeSHAP is
available); saliency or integrated gradients for the networks (the networks are not the project's model,
and permutation importance already compares the two families on equal terms); any method that needs the
test part.

## Which rows may be used

- **Validation rows (426, of which 97 resistant):** all contributions, all importances, the confidence
  zones and the example explanations. Validation is the part the protocol allows for choosing thresholds.
- **Training rows (2,977):** only the descriptive R-vs-S intensity contrast per region, and the
  shuffled-label control.
- **Test rows: not scored again.** The model was scored once, on 2026-09-18, and the probabilities were
  saved. The out-of-sample behaviour of the confidence zones is *derived from those stored probabilities*
  after asserting that they reproduce the logged test AUROC to 1e-12. No new row is appended to
  `results/experiments/test_evaluations.csv`, and the run asserts the file is unchanged when it finishes.

## Confidence zones (the rule, fixed here)

Three outputs instead of two, from the calibrated probability *p* and the model's cut-off *t* = 0.1426:

| Output | Condition |
|---|---|
| High-confidence susceptible | *p* < *l* |
| **Uncertain — conventional AST confirmation recommended** | *l* ≤ *p* ≤ *u* |
| High-confidence resistant | *p* > *u* |

The two edges are fitted on the validation part, each side on its own target, both **0.95**:

- *l* = the **largest** candidate cut with *l* ≤ *t* for which at least **95 %** of validation rows below it
  are truly susceptible, and which covers at least **5 %** of validation rows.
- *u* = the **smallest** candidate cut with *u* ≥ *t* for which at least **95 %** of validation rows above it
  are truly resistant, and which covers at least **5 %** of validation rows.

Candidates are the observed validation probabilities, so the rule has no free parameter beyond the two
targets and the coverage floor. Larger *l* and smaller *u* mean more confident calls, which is why the
extreme satisfying cut is taken on each side.

**Fallback, fixed in advance.** A target of 0.95 on the resistant side is demanding: at the model's
operating point only 33 % of the isolates it flags are in fact resistant. If a side cannot reach 0.95 at
5 % coverage, then **that zone does not exist**, the report says so plainly, and it states the highest
precision (or NPV) that side does reach at 5 % coverage, together with the full trade-off curve. The target
is not lowered after seeing the answer.

## Robustness checks

A region is only worth reporting if it is not an artefact of one fitted model. Three checks, all
pre-registered:

1. **Stability across seeds.** The five cached fits (seeds 42–46) of the same winning setting are already on
   disk. Each must first reproduce its own logged validation AUROC to 1e-12; then the rank agreement of
   their bin importances (Spearman, and overlap of the top 20 regions) is reported. If a cached fit cannot
   be verified it is dropped and the report says which.
2. **Shuffled-label control.** The winning setting is refitted once on the training rows with the labels
   permuted (seed 42), built through the same `base_pipeline`. The top-region importance of that model is
   the noise floor; the real model's top regions are reported against it.
3. **Cross-model agreement.** The same block permutation importance is run on the saved Version 0.5 MLP
   (a different model family, trained on 18 Da coarse bins). Agreement between two families that were fitted
   independently is evidence about the data; disagreement is evidence about the models. Either way it is
   reported.

And one model-free reference: for every reported region, the standardised mean difference of intensity
between resistant and susceptible **training** spectra. A region the model uses that shows no univariate
difference is being used in combination with others — worth knowing, not a defect.

## What may not be said about a region

**No m/z region is given a protein or peptide identity.** DRIAMS spectra are not identified proteomics;
this project has no MS/MS confirmation, no strain-matched reference spectra and no independent panel. The
reports therefore name regions by their m/z interval only. A sentence to that effect is generated into
every table file, so it cannot be dropped by accident. Nothing in Version 0.6 may be phrased as a
biological mechanism.

## Reporting

- Global importance per bin and per region, with direction, for the chosen model.
- Both permutation importances, the SHAP-vs-permutation agreement, the seed stability, the shuffled-label
  floor, the cross-model comparison with the MLP, and the R-vs-S contrast per region.
- The confidence zones: edges, coverage and error rate per zone on validation, and the same derived from the
  stored test probabilities with patient-group bootstrap intervals.
- Three individual explanations (a confident-susceptible row, an uncertain row and the highest-probability
  validation row), identified by validation row index only — never by spectrum code, patient, case or order
  number.
- **If the regions turn out to be unstable, or if no high-confidence zone exists on a side, the report says
  so plainly.** Both are legitimate findings about a model with AUROC 0.75, and neither is a reason to
  change a target or a method after the fact.
