# Discrimination audit: what could limit the ceftriaxone model, before any Version 1.4 decision

**Date: 2026-09-30.** Written after the Version 1.3 closure (`c5cfb49`) and before any Version 1.4 plan or code.
Development data, saved outputs, code and metadata only: nothing was fitted, nothing was scored, no test part was
read, and DRIAMS-C stayed closed. Every number below comes from
[`results/metrics/v1.4/audit/audit_tables.md`](../results/metrics/v1.4/audit/audit_tables.md), generated by
`scripts/discrimination_audit.py` at `4ecb130`. Patient keys were used in memory to link rows within a DRIAMS-A
year folder, as the dataset builder does, and were never written.

**Result.** No verified defect affects a historical conclusion. One constraint is verified and concrete: the
development pool holds only **105 resistant patients**, and a never-used set of excluded screening isolates would
add **233 new patients (229 of them resistant)** to training. That supports one bounded, development-only test;
nothing else found does.

## 2. Verified defects

None that affects a historical conclusion. The checks that would have found one: a transformation fitted outside
its training rows, a label mapping error, a duplicated spectrum or record, a patient group split between training
and evaluation within a year, a calibration or weighting step that alters ranking, and a period or sample-type
shortcut behind the pooled ranking. Each came back negative above.

## 3. Plausible hypotheses (none is promised to improve anything)

1. **Too few resistant patients to learn from (pursued).** The pool holds 105 resistant patients, about 84 in a
   training fold. The excluded screening isolates dated before 2018 hold 496 usable spectra (486 resistant; a
   label-free check removed 3 unreadable spectra and 7 whose raw and binned files differ), from 260 patient
   groups: 27 are pool patients (70 spectra), and **233 are new (426 spectra; 229 resistant)**. Another 8 spectra
   belong to patients whose clinical isolates are outside the pool and could not be used. They are colonisation
   isolates, not the clinical population, so they could only ever be *training* data.
   **The main risk.** 98 % of them are resistant, so in the added data "screening" and "resistant" coincide.
   Screening cultures probably grew under other conditions (DRIAMS does not record the medium), and culture
   medium changes MALDI-TOF peaks enough to affect strain-level differentiation
   ([Wieme et al., *Appl. Environ. Microbiol.* 2014](https://doi.org/10.1128/AEM.03708-13);
   [Topić Popović et al., *Mass Spectrom. Rev.* 2023](https://doi.org/10.1002/mas.21739)).
   A model could learn the source rather than resistance. An evaluation restricted to clinical isolates cannot be
   inflated by that; the addition could only be useless or harmful.
2. **Unequal patient contributions (not pursued).** Patient-balanced weights could change which strains dominate.
   Support is weak: the 0.016–0.028 gap between per-spectrum and first-spectrum AUROC is within fold noise and could
   equally reflect repeat measurements of easy strains or cross-year recurrence.
3. **Temporal drift (not testable cleanly).** Version 1.2's forward check (AUROC 0.69–0.72) fell below its random
   cross-validation (0.76–0.78), but its training part was half the size, so time and training size are confounded.
   A 2025 validation study found such models lose performance when training and test data differ in place or time
   ([Wiesmann et al., *J. Clin. Microbiol.* 2025](https://doi.org/10.1128/jcm.01186-25), with a published correction).
4. **Cross-year recurrence inflating random-split estimates (not testable).** Patient keys are re-hashed yearly.
5. **A biological ceiling (not testable here).** Ceftriaxone resistance in *E. coli* is mostly acquired
   β-lactamases, proteins larger than the 2–20 kDa range binned here, so the spectrum may reflect it mainly
   indirectly, through lineage. The published reference for this pair on DRIAMS-A, AUROC 0.74
   ([Weis et al., *Nat. Med.* 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-021-01619-9)),
   is close to this project's results; that is consistent with a ceiling, not evidence of one.

## 4. Explanations the data do not support

- That the model ranks by period or sample type (within-stratum AUROC equals pooled AUROC).
- That class imbalance or weighting is the constraint (balanced and unweighted searches agree).
- Leakage through preprocessing or a fitted transformation (none exists outside a training fold).
- Label artefacts (one I result; ambiguous results excluded; no conflicting duplicates).
- That a prevalence change explains the lost sensitivity (withdrawn in Version 1.2).
- That "the limit is discrimination" (withdrawn in the Version 1.3 closure: the tested rules did not establish
  useful performance, which is not a universal limit).

## 5. What a study on this pool can detect

Between two model settings on the same rows, the per-fold AUROC difference over Version 1.2's 15 folds had SD 0.019;
the corrected repeated-cross-validation 95 % interval (Nadeau–Bengio correction) has a half-width of about 0.022,
and a difference of about 0.03 is detectable with 80 % power. A change of training data will likely vary more. A null result on this
pool therefore means "no improvement of roughly that size was shown", not "no improvement".

## 6. Decision

**One bounded Version 1.4 study of hypothesis 1 is justified**, and only that. It names a concrete, never-used data
source; it addresses the one constraint the audit verified; it is a data change, not another search; and it can be
tested on the same development pool without any spent test part, 2018 data or DRIAMS-C, with evaluation on clinical
isolates only. Hypotheses 2–5 are recorded, not pursued. Had the audit found no such source, the recommendation
would have been to stop model iteration and write the reproducible research report — which is what the Version 1.4
plan's stopping rule requires after it, whatever it finds.
