## 1. Why this question

The audit found no defect that affects a historical conclusion, and no evidence that the model ranks by period,
sample type, class weight or a fitted transformation. It verified one constraint: the pool holds only **105
resistant patients** (about 84 in a training fold). It also found a never-used source of resistant isolates: 659
screening isolates at DRIAMS-A with a ceftriaxone result, 645 resistant, excluded from every cohort because
screening (colonisation) isolates are not the clinical population. Before 2018, 496 of them are usable, from **233
patients new to the pool (229 resistant)**. The question is whether they help the model rank *clinical* isolates.

## 2. The question

**Primary.** With the model setting, calibration, folds and everything else held fixed, does adding the eligible
screening isolates to the training data only (A1) improve the AUROC on held-out clinical isolates of the development
pool, compared with training on clinical isolates only (A0)?

**What an answer can mean.** A higher AUROC is improved *ranking*. It is not useful sensitivity and specificity, and
it is not clinical usefulness: no operating requirement has been specified for this research model, and this plan
adopts none. The operating-point results are secondary and descriptive.

**The known risk, stated before any result.** 98 % of the screening isolates are resistant, so in the added data
"screening" and "resistant" coincide; their cultures probably grew under other conditions (DRIAMS does not record
the medium), and culture medium changes MALDI-TOF peaks. A1 may learn the source rather than resistance. Because
evaluation is restricted to clinical isolates, that cannot inflate the primary endpoint; it can make A1 no better or
worse. A source diagnostic is reported (section 8, item 5) to help interpret the result, not to select anything.

## 3. The baseline, and why it is the strongest eligible one

- **Setting:** the Version 0.4 ciprofloxacin winner (300 trees, learning rate 0.1, 31 leaves, minimum leaf 10,
  column share 0.3, row share 1.0, L2 10, no class weights), read from its saved model card. It was chosen on
  ciprofloxacin labels and has never been selected on a ceftriaxone label; it was Version 1.1's arm F, Version 1.2's
  arm C and Version 1.3's model.
- **Why not Version 1.1's arm T:** T's setting was selected by cross-validation on the ceftriaxone labels of the
  `random` training part, which contains this pool, so its results on the pool are optimistic (Version 1.2:
  B 0.780 against C 0.767, difference not demonstrated). It is not eligible as a fair comparator here.
- **Calibration and cut-off:** Version 1.2 arm C's procedure: `fit_calibrated` (sigmoid on the out-of-fold
  predictions of 5 patient-grouped inner folds, seed 42, `ensemble=False`) on the whole outer-training part, and
  the cut-off chosen on `cross_fitted_probabilities` of that part.
- **Reproduction is required.** A0 is Version 1.2's arm C, so it must reproduce Version 1.2's saved held-out
  predictions (section 10, check 4). If it does not, the run stops: the baseline would not be the recorded one.

## 4. Data, prior use, patients and time

**Permitted.**
1. *Evaluation and clinical training:* the Version 1.2 pool — `ecoli_ceftriaxone`'s `random` training and
   validation rows that were never in an inspected test part and share no patient group with one (2,421 spectra,
   247 resistant; 1,189 patient groups, 105 resistant). Labels unchanged (R or I = 1). **Prior use:** they trained
   and validated Version 1.1's arm T (search, fit, cut-off), were the whole of Version 1.2 (including its forward
   check on 2017) and of Version 1.3, and informed the audit.
2. *Screening training rows:* DRIAMS-A *E. coli* metadata rows with workstation `HospitalHygiene`, a single-category
   ceftriaxone result (R, I or S), and an acquisition date before 2018-01-01, whose spectra pass the dataset
   builder's checks (readable raw file, agreement with the published DRIAMS binned file, not a duplicate). They
   are built with the unchanged preprocessing by
   `python scripts/build_dataset.py --antibiotic Ceftriaxone --keep-workstation HospitalHygiene --name
   ecoli_ceftriaxone_with_screening --report-version v1.4`, a dataset whose other rows must equal
   `ecoli_ceftriaxone`'s byte for byte (section 10, check 2). **Prior use:** no model has seen them. The audit read
   their aggregate label counts (645 R and 14 S in all; 486 R and 10 S among the usable ones), dates and patient
   links, and checked their spectra label-free.
3. The saved Version 1.2 held-out predictions, for the reproduction check only.

**Not permitted:** every test part of the `random`, `temporal`, `external` and `external_ab` splits and every
patient group with a row in one; anything dated 2018 or later; DRIAMS-B, -C and -D; screening rows of patients who
have a clinical row outside the pool; the 93 isolates with no ciprofloxacin result; any other workstation.

**Patients.** DRIAMS-A re-hashes patient identifiers every year, so patients are grouped within a year folder only,
exactly as the builder groups them. A screening row joins its patient's group: expected 27 pool patients (70
screening spectra) and 233 patients who have no clinical row in the pool (426 spectra; 229 resistant patients),
260 groups in all; 8 spectra of 5 patients whose clinical rows lie outside the pool are excluded. **Whenever a
patient's clinical spectra are held out, that patient's screening spectra are left out of training too.**
Screening spectra are **never evaluated**. Across years a patient cannot be linked, so the same person may appear
as a screening isolate in one year and a clinical isolate in the next, on opposite sides of a fold. This is
disclosed, not correctable, and it applies equally to the clinical pool.

**Time.** Everything used is dated before 2018-01-01; 2018 is the spent temporal test period. The primary is a
patient-grouped random partition of 2015-11 to 2017-12, as in Version 1.2. The forward secondary (section 8, item 4)
uses Version 1.2's forward split unchanged: fit on what is dated before 2017-01-01 (for A1, screening spectra dated
before 2017-01-01 too), evaluate on 2017. Like Version 1.2's, it has no label-availability gap; that keeps it
reproducible against Version 1.2 and is disclosed.

## 5. The two arms, exactly

For each outer fold (and the forward split):
- **A0:** `train` = the outer-training clinical rows, in Version 1.2's order; inner folds
  `grouped_folds(y, groups, 5, 42)`; `fit_calibrated(setting, X_train, y_train, inner, seed 42, sigmoid)`.
- **A1:** the same clinical rows followed by every eligible screening row whose patient group has no held-out row
  (and, for the forward split, dated before 2017-01-01); inner folds by the same call on this augmented set; the same
  `fit_calibrated`. The patient groups of joined screening rows are their pool patients' groups; new patients get
  new group numbers.

Held fixed: the setting and its seed, the features (6,000 bins, unchanged preprocessing), the calibration method,
the outer partitions (Version 1.2's `outer_folds` on the pool, seeds 42, 43 and 44, 5 folds, at least 30 resistant
held out), the inner-fold rule and the cut-off rule. **A1's probabilities are not calibrated for clinical isolates**
(its calibrator saw the screening rows), so no probability-quality endpoint is reported for either arm; a monotone
calibrator changes neither ranking nor which isolates a cut-off chosen on the same scale flags.

## 6. The primary endpoint and its uncertainty

For each of the 15 outer folds f (partitions 42, 43, 44 × 5 folds): d_f = AUROC(A1) − AUROC(A0) on the fold's
held-out clinical spectra (all spectra, each once). The estimate is the mean of the 15 d_f. The 95 % interval uses
the corrected repeated-cross-validation variance (Nadeau & Bengio, *Machine Learning* 2003; Bouckaert & Frank,
PAKDD 2004): mean ± t(0.975, 14) · √((1/15 + 1/4) · s²), with s² the sample variance of the d_f.

**What this interval includes.** It reflects variability from the choice of training and held-out patients,
approximately (the correction is a heuristic for the overlap of training sets). It does **not** include variation
of the model seed, which stays 42 as in every earlier version. The bootstrap intervals of section 8 hold the fitted
models fixed and include no fitting variability at all.

**Verdict, fixed now (exploratory in every case):**
1. **"improved ranking"** — the interval lies above 0;
2. **"worse ranking"** — the interval lies below 0;
3. **"not demonstrated"** — otherwise. With the audit's estimate of fold noise, roughly 0.03 is detectable with
   80 % power, likely more because a change of training data varies more; "not demonstrated" never means "no
   effect" or "equivalent".

No minimum effect size is set: any such number would be a research choice, and it would not turn ranking into
usefulness.

## 7. The cut-off, fixed now

Version 1.2 arm C's procedure, unchanged: the highest cut-off whose sensitivity on the selection set is at least
0.90 (`choose_threshold`), where the selection set is the outer-training part's **clinical** spectra with their
cross-fitted predictions (`cross_fitted_probabilities`; for A1 the inner models include the screening rows, but
only clinical spectra select); supported only if the selection set holds at least 50 resistant spectra. It is
applied to the held-out clinical spectra, on partition 42 only. The 0.90 is the protocol's research target, not a
clinical standard.

## 8. Secondary endpoints — exploratory, reported whatever they show

1. **Pooled ranking per partition:** each arm's pooled out-of-fold AUROC and PR-AUC for partitions 42, 43 and 44;
   for partition 42, A1 minus A0 with a paired patient-group bootstrap (2,000 replicates, seed 42).
2. **One spectrum per patient:** partition 42's AUROC on each patient group's earliest held-out spectrum, per arm,
   and the paired difference with the same bootstrap.
3. **Operating point (partition 42):** pooled delivered sensitivity, specificity, flag rate and precision per arm,
   the number of supported cut-offs, and paired differences with the bootstrap; each arm marked when its pooled
   specificity is below 0.20 ("flags nearly everyone", a research line). No usefulness is claimed for any value.
4. **Forward in time:** Version 1.2's split (fit before 2017-01-01, evaluate on 2017: 915 / 76 → 1,506 / 171);
   each arm's AUROC and the paired difference with the bootstrap.
5. **Source diagnostic:** among resistant spectra dated before 2018 (the pool's 247 clinical and the usable
   screening ones), the cross-validated AUROC of the same setting for telling screening from clinical source, with
   5 folds grouped by patient (seed 42) and a patient-group bootstrap interval. Descriptive only: a high value
   means the two sources are distinguishable (medium, lineage mix or both); it selects nothing.

## 9. What may and may not be said

- "Improved ranking" is the most any result here can say, and only as an exploratory development finding.
- Nothing here is operational or clinical success; no specificity requirement exists, and none will be chosen after
  the results.
- A positive primary does not show why (strain diversity, class balance, or anything else). The saved searches
  showed that class re-weighting alone did not change cross-validated AUROC, which makes re-balancing an unlikely
  sole explanation, not an excluded one.
- A negative or null primary says nothing about other data sources, models or representations.

## 11. Multiplicity, failures and negative results

One primary comparison; everything else is labelled exploratory where it appears, and nothing is adjusted. A run
that stops early is logged as failed with its reason and reports nothing as a result; a rerun after fixing a defect
is a new run, and both are kept. Negative and null results are reported with the same prominence as positive ones.

## 12. Candidate count and budget

One candidate (A1) against one baseline (A0), nothing else. Fits: 3 partitions × 5 folds × 2 arms × 6
(`fit_calibrated`: 5 inner and 1 final) = 180; partition 42's cross-fitted cut-offs, 5 × 2 × 5 = 50; the forward
split, 2 × 6 = 12; the source diagnostic, 5. About 250 in all, estimated under 1 hour; **hard cap 2 hours**, beyond
which the run is recorded as failed.

## 13. Stopping rule, fixed now

**Version 1.4 is the last model-iteration study on this development pool, whatever it finds.** No further settings,
weights, representations, feature sets or data additions are tried on it, and a disappointing result is not
answered with an unplanned search.

- **"not demonstrated" or "worse ranking":** stop model iteration and prepare the reproducible research report of
  Versions 0.1–1.4.
- **"improved ranking":** freeze A1's procedure as recorded here and report it as an exploratory development
  finding in the research report. A confirmatory test would need data no version has used, and a separate,
  owner-approved amendment; DRIAMS-C's ceftriaxone labels are the only candidate, their standing is uncertain
  (`docs/driams_c_status.md`), and whether C's metadata can tell screening isolates apart is unknown. Version 1.4
  would recommend that the owner consider it only if A1 also does not flag nearly everyone at its operating point
  (partition 42's pooled specificity at least 0.20). No DRIAMS-C label is opened in Version 1.4.

## 15. Verification fixed in advance

Section 10's seven checks, asserted in code and tested on synthetic data, plus: the full suite and the pinned Ruff
pass at the run's commit; the run is from a clean tree.

## 16. What Version 1.4 cannot establish

That screening isolates would help at another site, in 2018 or later, at DRIAMS-C or in use; that any operating point
is useful or clinically acceptable; why a ranking change happened; or anything about ciprofloxacin.
