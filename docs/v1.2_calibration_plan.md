## 1. Why this question — the evidence

- **The operating point does not carry.** The protocol's rule — the highest cut-off with validation
  sensitivity ≥ 0.90 — delivered 0.827 on test for ciprofloxacin (Version 0.4) and 0.823 for ceftriaxone
  (Version 1.1). The Version 1.1 cut-off rested on 34 resistant validation spectra.
- **The probabilities barely beat the base rate.** Version 1.1's ceftriaxone Brier score was 0.083 against
  0.084 for always predicting the training resistance rate, with a calibration slope of 0.67.
- **More search was not the lever.** Arm F (the Version 0.4 ciprofloxacin setting, no search) was not
  demonstrated worse than arm T (the Version 1.1 search) on AUROC, and was better on PR-AUC and Brier
  (exploratory, not pre-registered).

So the question is not "which hyperparameters", but whether a simpler existing model, calibrated and
thresholded on more of the data it already has, gives better probabilities and a more reliable operating
point.

## 2. The question

**Primary.** On development data only: does **procedure C** — the Version 0.4 ciprofloxacin LightGBM setting,
fixed with no search, calibrated as in Version 0.4 on its whole training part, with its cut-off chosen on the
cross-fitted (out-of-fold) predictions of that whole part — give **better probability quality (a lower Brier
score)** on held-out patients than **procedure B**, the unchanged Version 1.1 procedure?

**Key secondary.** Is C's operating point more stable across held-out folds, and closer to its 90 %
sensitivity target, than B's?

## 3. What this can and cannot establish

**It can** establish, within DRIAMS-A spectra from 2015–2017: whether C's cross-validated probabilities are
better than B's; how often each procedure's cut-off delivers its target on held-out patients, and how much it
varies; and whether either is better than a no-skill prediction.

**It cannot** establish anything confirmatory: every spectrum used has been available to earlier versions,
and the question itself was prompted by Version 1.1's test results. It says nothing about 2018, another
site, another antibiotic, or clinical use, and nothing about whether C would beat B on an independent cohort.
The settings are fixed, so the result is conditional on them — and **B's setting was selected in Version 1.1
by cross-validation on data that overlaps P with the same labels**, which makes B look better than it would on
new data. That optimism favours the comparator, so it can only make a "C better" result harder to reach.

## 5. Cohort, labels, folds and leakage checks

- **Dataset and labels unchanged:** `ecoli_ceftriaxone`, R or I = 1, S = 0, every Version 1.1 exclusion.
- **P is derived, not drawn:** `random.train ∪ random.validation`, minus every row of an inspected test part,
  minus every row whose patient group touches one. Its row list is fingerprinted into every output.
- **Outer folds:** `StratifiedGroupKFold(5)`, groups = `group_id`, stratified on the label, for partition
  seeds 42, 43 and 44. Seed 42 is primary; 43 and 44 are a sensitivity analysis.
- **Inside each outer-training fold:** the Version 0.4 inner folds (`grouped_folds`, 5 folds, seed 42), the
  Version 0.4 calibrated fit (`fit_calibrated`, sigmoid, `ensemble=False`), model seed 42. Preprocessing is
  stateless; every fitted step — model, calibrator, cut-off — sees outer-training rows only.
- **Checked at run time; the run stops if any fails:** P shares no row and no patient group with a spent part;
  outer training and held-out folds share no patient group; B's validation slice lies inside the
  outer-training fold and is patient-grouped; no held-out row is passed to any fit, calibration or cut-off
  selection; C's out-of-fold predictions, mapped through its fitted calibrator, reproduce that model's own
  calibration.

## 6. The arms

| Arm | Model setting | Fitted on | Cut-off chosen on |
|---|---|---|---|
| **R0** no-skill | predicts the outer-training resistance rate | — | — |
| **B** unchanged Version 1.1 procedure (comparator) | Version 1.1 arm T (`best_model_card.json` → `params`) | 7/8 of the outer-training fold | the other 1/8: a patient-grouped validation slice, as in Version 1.1 |

B against D1 isolates the procedure with the setting held fixed; D1 against C isolates the setting with the
procedure held fixed. **No new search, no new model family, no network, no ensemble, no other antibiotic.**

## 7. The cut-off rule, with its uncertainty

1. **Rule (unchanged from the protocol):** the highest cut-off whose sensitivity on the selection set is at
   least 0.90.
2. **Support:** the target is *supported* only if the selection set holds **at least 50 resistant spectra**.
   The 95 % Wilson interval for an observed sensitivity of 0.90 is about 0.73–0.97 with 25 resistant, 0.79–0.96
   with 50, and 0.83–0.95 with 100; below 50 the rule cannot tell 0.90 from 0.80. An unsupported cut-off is
   still computed and applied, so its results can be reported, but they are labelled **"target not supported
   by the selection data"** and are never described as targeting 0.90. At P's size B's slice is expected to
   hold about 25 resistant spectra, and C's selection set about 200; this is a consequence of the design, known
   before any result.
3. **Reported with every cut-off:** the selection set's resistant count, its sensitivity at the cut-off with
   the Wilson interval, and the held-out sensitivity it actually delivered.

## 8. Endpoints and what may be said (fixed now)

**Primary.** Brier score of the pooled held-out predictions of the seed-42 partition: **B minus C**, with a
paired bootstrap of patient groups (2,000 resamples, seed 42, 95 % percentile interval).
- Interval entirely above 0: **"C gave better probabilities than B on development data (exploratory)".**
- Interval containing 0: **"not demonstrated"** — never "equivalent".
- Interval entirely below 0: **"C gave worse probabilities than B"**.

**Secondary — all exploratory, reported whatever they show:**
1. Brier skill against R0 for B, C and D1 (pooled, seed-42 partition, with intervals).
2. AUROC and PR-AUC (pooled, with intervals), and C minus B.
3. Calibration slope and intercept, and log loss (pooled).
4. **Operating-point stability** over the 15 held-out folds (5 folds × 3 partitions): delivered sensitivity —
   mean, SD, minimum, maximum, and how many folds reach ≥ 0.90 and ≥ 0.85 — plus specificity, the cut-off's
   spread, and each fold's support flag. **"C more stable than B"** is said only if C's SD of delivered
   sensitivity is smaller **and** its mean is closer to 0.90; it is a description, not a test.
5. Delivered sensitivity and specificity of the pooled seed-42 predictions, with intervals.
6. **Forward in time:** each arm fitted on P's spectra dated before 2017-01-01 (915; 76 resistant) and
   evaluated once on P's 2017 spectra (1,506; 171 resistant): Brier, AUROC, calibration slope, delivered
   sensitivity and specificity, with intervals. Patients cannot be linked across years (section 4).
7. D1 minus B and C minus D1 on Brier and AUROC (paired, seed-42 partition).
8. Partitions 43 and 44: the primary difference repeated, reported as mean and range.

The bootstrap treats each fold's fitted model as fixed, so the intervals leave out model-fitting
variability; the three partitions give a partial view of it.

## 9. Multiplicity, too few resistant, failures and negative results

- **One primary endpoint, one comparison.** Nothing else is adjusted or treated as confirmatory; each
  secondary number is labelled exploratory where it appears.
- **Too few resistant:** if any held-out fold holds fewer than 30 resistant spectra, the run stops as a design
  failure and is reported as one. The forward-in-time check needs at least 50 resistant in the evaluation part
  (171 are there); its cut-offs follow the support rule of section 7.
- **Failed runs:** a run that stops early is recorded in the development log as failed, with the reason. Its
  partial numbers are not reported as results. A rerun after fixing a code defect is a new run; both are kept.
- **Negative results** are reported at the same prominence as positive ones. A "not demonstrated" primary
  answers the question for this data; the endpoint is not switched afterwards, and no further arm is added.

## 10. Compute budget

Fixed settings, no search: 3 partitions × 5 folds × (B, C, D1), plus the forward-in-time check. Estimated
at about 30–45 minutes on this laptop; hard cap 3 hours. If the cap is exceeded the run is recorded as failed.
The number of partitions is not reduced after a run starts.

## 12. The only route to a future confirmatory claim

A later one-time held-out evaluation needs **all** of the following, decided before any of its data is
touched:

1. **Data that no version has scored for this label.** The only candidate found is DRIAMS-C's ceftriaxone
   labels. Using them needs a dated amendment, approved by the owner, that explicitly reopens DRIAMS-C for the
   ceftriaxone label only and states that its spectra were used for ciprofloxacin in Version 0.8 (adaptation
   fits on 70 %, one scoring of the protected 30 %).
2. **A cohort fixed in advance:** built with the same rules, its eligibility (≥ 30 per class) checked from label
   counts only, its shared spectra byte-identical to the existing site-C dataset.
3. **One frozen procedure:** C only if this plan's primary endpoint says "C better"; otherwise the unchanged
   Version 1.1 procedure — or no evaluation. The training data, settings and cut-off rule are fixed and hashed
   before scoring.
4. **Endpoints and success rules fixed in that amendment**, one scoring through the strict gate, and a pre-score
   record committed before any row is loaded.

Nothing in Version 1.2 is confirmatory, and none of its numbers may be described as independent evidence.

## Correction — 2026-09-30: three statements in the record and the README

Appended; the record above is left as written.

1. **A causal claim is withdrawn.** The README said every arm under-predicted 2017 "so fewer resistant isolates
   crossed cut-offs set on the earlier period", and the working report said the study had found *why* the
   sensitivity target is missed. Neither is supported. The data show two observations beside the sensitivity
   loss — resistance was more common in the later year, and every arm under-predicted it on average — but a
   change in prevalence alone (pure label shift) would leave sensitivity at a fixed cut-off unchanged, and a
   positive calibration intercept fits several kinds of shift. Label shift was not established, and the design
   cannot separate a change in how resistant spectra score from the noise of cut-offs chosen on at most 76
   resistant spectra, or from the rule's own tendency to fall short.
2. **An attribution is corrected.** The README said "C's procedure improved Brier slightly (B minus D1:
   +0.0020)". The arithmetic and sign are right — Brier(B) minus Brier(D1) = 0.08021 − 0.07825 = +0.00196, so
   D1's probabilities were slightly better — but no cut-off enters a Brier score, so the difference can only come
   from fitting on 8/8 rather than 7/8 of each training fold, not from the cross-fitted cut-off.
3. **Rounding.** Three README figures were rounded from `tables.md`'s four decimals instead of the complete
   report's three: B's Brier skill is 0.124 (not 0.125), D1's delivered sensitivity 0.874 (0.819–0.923), and
   C's forward delivered sensitivity 0.789.

`tables_complete.md` (from `scripts/v12_report.py`, saved outputs only — no model rerun, no prediction
regenerated) now tabulates every endpoint of section 8, including the three the run's `tables.md` omitted, with
the operands of every difference named. DRIAMS-C's standing is audited in `docs/driams_c_status.md`.
