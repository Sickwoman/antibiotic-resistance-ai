## 1. Why this question

Four times the protocol's cut-off rule — the highest cut-off with selection sensitivity at least 0.90 — has
delivered less than 0.90 on data it did not choose on: 0.827 (ciprofloxacin, Version 0.4 test), 0.823
(ceftriaxone, Version 1.1 test), a mean of 0.892 over Version 1.2's held-out folds, and 0.556–0.789 in Version
1.2's forward-in-time split. The rule targets 0.90 on its selection data exactly, so even without any shift it
falls short about half the time; and it attaches no uncertainty to the target. The question is whether a rule
that does account for the selection sample's uncertainty holds the target better in later periods, and at what
cost in specificity.

## 2. The question

**Primary.** With the model and calibration held fixed, does the order-statistic rule **U** deliver the 90 %
sensitivity target in later development periods more often than the empirical rule **E**, and what does it cost
in specificity? No specificity requirement is operationally justified (section 6), so this is a
sensitivity–specificity trade-off study: it can find that U delivers higher sensitivity, and whether it does so
without flagging nearly everyone, but it cannot declare operational success.

**Separate, exploratory.** Does updating only the calibration intercept on the most recent six months of labels
available before an origin improve the probability quality of that later period? This changes no cut-off
decision (section 8), and it is kept apart from the primary.

## 3. The baseline, and its history

- **Model setting:** the Version 0.4 ciprofloxacin winner (300 trees, learning rate 0.1, 31 leaves, minimum
  leaf 10, column share 0.3, row share 1.0, L2 10, no class weights). It was chosen in Version 0.4 by
  cross-validated AUROC on **ciprofloxacin** labels (a 30-draw search); it has never been selected on a
  ceftriaxone label. It was Version 1.1's arm F (test parts scored once) and Version 1.2's arm C. The Version 1.1
  arm T setting is not used: it was selected on the ceftriaxone labels of this same pool.
- **Calibration:** the Version 0.4 procedure (`fit_calibrated`: sigmoid on the out-of-fold predictions of 5
  patient-grouped inner folds, seed 42, `ensemble=False`), unchanged since Version 0.4.
- **Rule E:** the protocol's rule, approved 2026-09-17 — the highest cut-off with selection sensitivity at least
  0.90 (`choose_threshold`), applied to the same selection sample as U.

## 4. Data, periods and label availability

The pool is Version 1.2's: `ecoli_ceftriaxone`'s `random` training and validation rows that were never in an
inspected test part and share no patient group with one. Labels unchanged (R or I = 1).

**Prior use of these labels.** They trained and validated Version 1.1's arm T (search, fit, cut-off), were the
whole of Version 1.2, and **the 2017 part was evaluated in aggregate by Version 1.2's forward check** (a model

- **Label availability.** A label counts as available once 7 days have passed after its spectrum's acquisition
  date; susceptibility testing takes up to about 72 hours. Spectra in the gap are used nowhere. No correction,
  cut-off or fit ever uses a label from the period it is evaluated on.
- **Patients.** DRIAMS-A re-hashes patient identifiers every year. Within a year, a patient group already seen
  before an origin has its later-period spectra removed from evaluation. **Across the 2016/2017 boundary a
  patient cannot be linked**, so the same person may appear on both sides of the first origin, and in both years
  of the second origin's selection sample. This is disclosed, not corrected.
- **Repeat spectra.** The 247 resistant spectra come from 105 resistant patient groups (2.35 each). The selection
  sample therefore keeps **one spectrum per patient group**: the earliest-dated, then the lowest row number.
  Evaluation keeps all spectra and resamples whole patient groups.

## 5. The model and the selection scores

At each origin: fit the fixed setting with the fixed calibration on all fit data; the **selection scores** are
the cross-fitted predictions of that same fit data (each spectrum predicted by an inner-fold model that did not
train on it), mapped through the fitted calibrator — Version 1.2's `cross_fitted_probabilities`, which proves
they reproduce the calibrator — and then reduced to one per patient group. The final model's predictions on the
later period are what the cut-offs are applied to. A held-out selection window was considered and rejected
before this plan: the six months before each origin hold only 18 and 26 resistant patient groups, below the 29
that rule U needs.

## 6. Specificity: there is no justified operational floor

No clinical workflow, cost model or user requirement has been specified for this research model, and the
project may not invent one. **Version 1.3 declares no operational success.** One line is fixed only to name
uselessness: a cut-off with **specificity below 0.20** flags at least 80 % of susceptible isolates — at these data's
resistance of about 10 %, roughly four in five of all isolates — and is labelled **"flags nearly everyone:
unhelpful under any operating requirement"**. Staying above 0.20 does not make a cut-off useful.

## 7. The two rules, exactly

Let the selection sample hold `n` resistant patient groups with scores `r(1) ≤ … ≤ r(n)`. Both rules flag an
isolate as resistant when its predicted probability is **at or above** the cut-off, so ties at the cut-off are
flagged.

- **E (existing):** the highest cut-off whose sensitivity on the selection sample is at least 0.90 — in effect
  `r(m + 1)` with `m = floor(0.1 n)` misses allowed.
- **U (order-statistic tolerance rule):** `k* = max{k : P(Binomial(n, 0.10) ≥ k) ≥ 0.95}`, and the cut-off is
  `r(k*)`. If no `k` qualifies — which happens exactly when `n < 29` — U is **infeasible** for that origin and
  gives no cut-off. At `n = 39`, `k* = 1` (the cut-off is the lowest resistant selection score); at `n = 63`,
  `k* = 3`.

**Why U, and what it does and does not guarantee.** If the `n` resistant scores are independent draws from the
distribution that future resistant scores come from, and the scoring function did not depend on them, then
`F(r(k))` has a Beta(`k`, `n − k + 1`) distribution, so `P(true sensitivity ≥ 0.90) = P(Binomial(n, 0.10) ≥ k)`,
and U makes that at least 0.95. This is the nonparametric tolerance-limit argument used by the Neyman–Pearson
"umbrella" algorithm (Tong, Feng & Li, *Science Advances* 2018; 4: eaao1659), applied to missed resistant
isolates. U can be described as "the highest cut-off whose one-sided 95 % Clopper–Pearson lower bound on selection
sensitivity is at least 0.90" — for tie-free scores the two coincide — but its validity comes from the order-
statistic argument, which already accounts for the cut-off being chosen from the data. **A lower confidence
bound computed after a free search over cut-offs would not, in general, be a valid guarantee; U is not that.**

**In this design the guarantee is a heuristic, for three stated reasons:**
1. *Exchangeability fails by design:* later periods may differ from earlier ones — which is exactly what is
   being tested — so no bound is claimed for them.
2. *The scores are cross-fitted:* they come from inner-fold models trained on about 80 % of the fit data, while
   the cut-off is applied to the final model trained on all of it. Even with no shift the 95 % is approximate.
3. *Independence is approximate:* one spectrum per patient group within a year, but patients cannot be linked
   across 2016/2017.

Version 1.3 therefore **measures delivered sensitivity; it claims no bound.**

## 8. Endpoints and what may be said (fixed now)

**Primary.** Pooled delivered sensitivity over the two later periods (each spectrum once, each period cut by the
rules from its own origin), **U minus E**, with a **two-level patient-group bootstrap** (2,000 replicates, seed 42).
In each replicate and for each origin: resample the selection sample's resistant and susceptible patient groups
separately, with replacement, keeping their counts; recompute both cut-offs; resample the later period's patient
groups with replacement; compute delivered sensitivity and specificity; pool the two periods. Replicates in
which an evaluation resample holds a single class are skipped and counted. The models are held fixed, so fitting
variability is left out. Because U's cut-off is never above E's, U minus E is never negative in any replicate;
the interval asks whether it is reliably above zero.

**Verdict, applied in this order:**
1. **"infeasible"** — U has no cut-off at an origin (fewer than 29 resistant patient groups). Not expected: the
   counts are 39 and 63.
2. **"not demonstrated"** — the 95 % interval of U minus E includes 0.
3. **"higher sensitivity only by flagging nearly everyone: unhelpful"** — U's pooled specificity is below 0.20.
4. **"U improves sensitivity but does not reach 0.90"** — U's pooled delivered sensitivity is below 0.90.
5. **"both reach 0.90; U only adds specificity cost"** — E's pooled delivered sensitivity is also at least 0.90.
6. **"U improves attainment of the 90 % target (exploratory)"** — otherwise.

None of these is operational success; each is reported with the specificity it costs.

**Secondary — exploratory, reported whatever they show:**
1. Per period: each rule's cut-off, selection `n`, `k*` or `m`, delivered sensitivity and specificity with
   two-level intervals, flag rate and precision, and the "flags nearly everyone" marker.
2. **Attainment probability:** the share of bootstrap replicates in which each rule's delivered sensitivity is
   at least 0.90, per period and pooled. It describes the resampling distribution; it is not a guarantee.
3. The specificity cost, E minus U, and the flag-rate difference, paired, with intervals.
4. Each rule's sensitivity on its own selection sample beside what it delivered later.

**The separate recalibration question (exploratory; probability quality only).** At each origin, fit a single
intercept `d` by maximum likelihood so that `logit(p) + d` matches the labels of the most recent six months
before the gap — using those spectra's cross-fitted predictions — and apply it to the later period's final-model
predictions. Endpoints: the later period's Brier score and calibration intercept, recalibrated minus uncorrected,
with an evaluation-group bootstrap. It needs at least 30 resistant spectra and 15 resistant patient groups in
the recent window; the counts are 40 / 18 and 65 / 24. **It changes no cut-off decision:** a monotone map
preserves order, so any cut-off re-derived on the recalibrated scale flags the same isolates.

## 9. Multiplicity, support, infeasibility, failures and negative results

- One primary comparison; everything else is labelled exploratory where it appears, and nothing is adjusted.
- The feasibility counts in section 4 were computed before this plan was written, from labels and dates only; no
  model had been fitted. If the run's counts differ from them, it stops.
- An infeasible U is reported as infeasible; the target, the confidence and the rule are **not** relaxed to make
  it feasible.
- A run that stops early is logged as failed with its reason and reports nothing as a result; a rerun after
  fixing a defect is a new run, and both are kept.
- Negative results are reported at the same prominence as positive ones, always with their specificity.
